# `desktop-ledger-editor` plan

## Description

Amended 2026-11-06: P05 added on BOSS ruling; retirement of the unreachable prototype; deferral is sequencing, not waiver.

Approved 2026-10-23. Basis: in session CADRUMO-UX-LEDGER the owner asked for a table-based desktop batch editor for movements, invoices and categorisation, at parity with the CLI and TUI ledger. They asked for the interface to be prototyped, adversarially critiqued and improved before implementation, and said the work needs no decision from them.

The work adds a Ledger page to the desktop shell and a host channel for the ledger family's registered operations. The decision is `2026-10-23-desktop-ledger-editor-adr`, grounded in `2026-10-23-desktop-ledger-editor-reference`. That ADR governs P01 (the host channel) and bounds every Step that submits an operation. The design system rules come from `2026-10-05-desktop-design-system-adr` (P02, P03, P04). The shell rules on storage, keys and strings come from `2026-10-04-desktop-shell-adr` (P03, P04). The ledger data contracts (`2026-06-10-ledger-interface-contract-adr`, `2026-06-10-ledger-amount-direction-adr`, `2026-06-10-ledger-filter-period-adr`, `2026-06-30-ledger-add-idempotency-adr`, `2026-07-05-ledger-latency-budget-adr`) bound the P03 editors without new decisions. `2026-06-10-ledger-input-localization-adr` governs the CLI entry boundary and does not bind the window's editors, which follow `2026-10-05-desktop-design-system-adr`. The interaction design is the reviewed prototype, kept with the session's design notes. No other costly decision is involved. A product batch operation for corrections is out of scope (see the ADR's consequences).

**Amendment basis and P05 scope.** The design prototype under `native/desktop/frontend/src/dev/prototypes/ledger/` and its stylesheet `native/desktop/frontend/src/styles/ledger-prototype.css` were reachable only through Storybook, carried hardcoded tax data and years that the frontend must not hold, and held seven modules over the 1,500-line module bound. They are retired in the commit after this amendment. Seven capabilities existed only there. Three are binding rulings of `2026-10-24-desktop-ledger-editor-audit` (R19, R20 and the shortcuts sheet of M2); the others are partial production behaviour. P05 records each as an open Step. Deferring them is sequencing, not a waiver of the audit's rulings. Git history is not the specification: the paragraphs below are. Every P05 Step is owned by FRONTEND. Every visible word comes from the backend or from the strings catalogue declared in `dev/locales/desktop_chrome.py` in four locales; the prototype's English copy below only describes intent and is not to be copied. No Step introduces a year, a rate, a category code or any other tax fact in frontend code, fixtures or stories. Decision coverage: `2026-10-23-desktop-ledger-editor-adr`, its 2026-10-24 amendment on model reviews, and `2026-10-05-desktop-design-system-adr` cover P05, except where a Step below names a host contract addition; that addition is assessed against the ADR before the Step starts and raised to the orchestrator if it is not covered.

**P05.S20 - phone width (audit R19, `2026-10-24-desktop-ledger-editor-audit` lines 317-318).** The main window's minimum width is 520px (`native/desktop/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json.in:14`), so the phone layout is reachable. Below 47.5rem (the audit's 760px), the prototype did this. The header's section tabs and Setup menu became one native select, labelled as the Ledger section, with the primary sections as options and the Setup sections in an option group. The header's action buttons collapsed to icon buttons; the keyboard-shortcuts button and the maximize control were hidden. The readiness strip stayed above the list as one horizontally scrolling row, followed by the banners and the toolbar, with search taking the full width. The grid was replaced by a list of cards labelled Movements. Unsaved new movements came first, each a card with a "new" tag and its description. Each stored movement was one card: a main button holding a screen-reader-only status word, the description (truncated), a muted second line with date and counterparty, and at the right the amount with its currency and the direction mark; beside it, a classification chip showing the staged-aware classification with the unsaved marker. A staged card carried the R1 dashed leading bar. Activating a card or its chip selected that movement alone, put the active cell on classification and opened the inspector as a bottom sheet: fixed to the bottom, full width, 72dvh tall, rounded top corners, a handle bar, the resize handle hidden, over a scrim; tapping the scrim or the inspector's close closed it. The To match side list was hidden and that view fell back to cards. The empty-period state stayed as it is. The review dialog went full width, its before-and-after rows narrowed their label column, and two-column dialog layouts stacked into one column. The period selector kept its component with a tighter trigger. R19 also requires shortcut hints to be hidden on touch: hide chord hints in tooltips when the primary pointer is coarse. Production must use the page's paged window, not the prototype's fixed cap of 120 cards. Prototype stories: "Phone, movements as cards", "Phone, a movement's sheet", "Phone, review before saving", "Phone, period selector" (Storybook mobile viewport). Insertion points: `native/desktop/frontend/src/components/ledger/movements/MovementsSection.tsx` (branch before the grid), a new card list module in `components/ledger/movements/`, `components/ledger/LedgerHeader.tsx` (section select, hidden controls), `components/ledger/inspector/RightPanel.tsx` (sheet mode), `components/ledger/editing/ReviewDialog.tsx` and `components/ledger/PeriodSelector.tsx`. Acceptance: Storybook stories at a phone viewport for the four states; a scenario test at a width below the breakpoint opens a card's sheet, stages a classification from it and closes it with focus returned to the card; axe passes in light and dark.

**P05.S21 - first-run checklist (audit R20, lines 320-321).** The prototype showed it in place of the Movements body when the ledger held no stored movement in any period and no unsaved new movement; a period that is merely empty keeps today's empty state (`components/ledger/LedgerPageBody.tsx:203-235`). Layout: a centred column of about 40rem, a short heading and a one-line lead saying nothing leaves the computer, then an ordered list of three raised cards, each with a step number badge (replaced by a tick when the step is done), a bold title, one muted line and one button. Step 1, register your bank accounts: the button opens Registers on its accounts pane; it is ticked when at least one own account exists, and its line then says how many are registered. Step 2, import a statement: the primary button opens the Import dialog; its line names the accepted formats, which production must take from the import capability, not from a literal list. Step 3, review what the rules caught: the button opens Rules, and its line points at the needs-review view. Production test for "no movement at all": the served years-with-movements read (`useLedgerYears` in `components/ledger/useLedgerPage.ts:208`) is empty and no draft is staged; never a year comparison. Buttons reuse the page's held actions so the write pause disables Import exactly as in the empty state; accounts come from `useOwnAccounts`. Prototype story: "State, first run: nothing imported yet". Acceptance: a story for the first run and for the accounts step done; a scenario test shows the checklist on an empty ledger and the grid after an import; R22's copy budget holds.

**P05.S22 - keyboard-shortcuts sheet (audit M2, lines 383-394; R16, line 263; R17 chord hints, line 301).** The prototype opened a wide dialog titled Keyboard shortcuts from a keyboard icon button in the page header (hint "?"), from a palette entry, and from the typed "?" key when focus was outside the grid with neither Ctrl nor Alt held. Its lead said the grid works without a mouse and that the window keeps its own chords, which the page never takes. It listed groups (Move, Select, Edit with "staged until you save", Headers, Page), each a definition list of key chips, alternatives joined by "or", and a one-line description. The prototype's list is wrong against M2 and must not be copied: it listed bare letters (N, B/P/M, "/"). Production lists only the chords the page and the grid actually declare, rendered from their declarations with `chordText` so the shown key is the working key: F8 and Shift+F8 (next and previous readiness issue, `components/ledger/movements/MovementsSection.tsx:663`), Alt+B/P/M (`components/ledger/movements/useClassifyChords.ts`), Alt+I, Ctrl+Enter, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y (`components/ledger/ledgerChords.ts`), Ctrl+Alt+Z (`components/ledger/editing/MovementEditing.tsx:137`), and the grid primitive's keyboard model (`components/ui/data-grid.tsx`: arrows, Home/End, Ctrl+Home/End, PageUp/PageDown, F2, Enter, typing to edit, Escape, Space, Shift+arrows, Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V, Delete, ArrowUp from the first row to the headers, header Enter/Space, Ctrl+Left/Right, Alt+Left/Right, Shift+F10 and the Menu key). The precedent is the profile page's key sheet (`components/profile/ProfileDialogs.tsx:31-80`) and its typed, layout-independent help key (`components/profile/profileKeys.ts:17`). Insertion points: a new `components/ledger/LedgerKeysDialog.tsx`, the button in `components/ledger/LedgerHeader.tsx`, the palette entry and the page-level "?" listener in `components/ledger/useLedgerPage.ts`. Hidden at phone width (P05.S20). Prototype story: "Keyboard shortcuts". Acceptance: a unit test asserts that every chord the sheet lists is a declared page or grid chord, that every declared page chord is listed, and that none is a shell chord; a scenario test shows "?" typed in a grid cell starts editing rather than opening the sheet; `aria-keyshortcuts` on the header button.

**P05.S23 - inspector for an unsaved new movement (audit R4, lines 159-160; R11, line 227; R18).** Production drafts new movements into the grid (`components/ledger/movements/MovementsSection.tsx:389-404`), but the inspector ignores them: `components/ledger/inspector/LedgerInspector.tsx` keeps only stored transaction ids and shows "nothing selected". The prototype's draft inspector (`Inspector.tsx:2592`, retired) showed: a header titled by the draft's description or a "new movement" word, subtitled as a draft not saved yet; a warning alert listing the required fields still missing, or a success alert saying it is added when the changes are saved; an editable field list with required fields marked; a hint that a group label and documents are added after saving and that a retry never adds the movement twice; a Technical details disclosure with the add operation id and the draft's idempotency key (R18); and a Remove draft button. Production derives the fields from `DraftField` (`shell/ledgerStage.ts:58`) and the required set from the add operation's contract, never from the prototype's list; edits stage `edit_draft` and removal stages `remove_draft`; direction and account start empty with the "no account" option (R4); an unknown add keeps the draft and its key (R11). Insertion points: a draft branch in `components/ledger/inspector/LedgerInspector.tsx` keyed by the grid's draft key, and a new `components/ledger/inspector/DraftInspector.tsx` reusing `InspectorField.tsx`. There was no dedicated prototype story; it was reached from Add movement. Add an inspector story for a new movement next to the production story `AddingMovement` (`components/ledger/LedgerPage.stories.tsx:206`). Acceptance: a scenario test adds a movement, fills the required fields in the inspector, sees the alert change, saves, and sees the stored row replace the draft.

**P05.S24 - locked, never-read and stale ledger states (audit R22 empty and error states, line 358; R11 re-read).** Production shows loading, failed, empty and signed-out only (`components/ledger/LedgerPageBody.tsx:166-235`). In the prototype (`App.tsx:1237-1260`, `Movements.tsx:3424`, `3653`, `3748`, retired), locked and never read replaced every section with an empty state: an unplugged-connection icon, a one-line title and one line of detail (locked: another window is resetting the ledger; never read: nothing has been read in this window), and one Retry button that re-reads with a pending spinner; while locked, never read, unavailable or loading, Add movement was disabled. Stale did not block: a warning banner with a clock icon said how long ago the ledger was read and that it may be stale, with a Re-read button; the readiness strip's tooltip said when it was last checked; a re-read cleared the banner. The ledger port's failure codes have no such states (`native/desktop/frontend/src/shell/ledger.ts:337-348`), while the shell's Home and Declarations reads serve exactly `available | locked | stale | never_captured | unavailable` (`shell/modeloContract/modeloDeclarations.ts:76`, `88`). Acceptance: the three states come from a served availability on the ledger read and the age from a served read time, never from a client clock threshold; if the ledger read serves no availability, the Step first adds it through the host channel, after the decision-coverage check named above. Prototype stories: "State, locked", "State, never read", "State, stale". Insertion points: `components/ledger/LedgerPageBody.tsx`, `components/ledger/movements/MovementWindowNotices.tsx` for the stale banner, `components/ledger/useLedgerPage.ts` for the disabled actions. Acceptance tests: a story and a scenario test per state, including Retry and Re-read.

**P05.S25 - palette year search (audit R16, line 263; R21, lines 323-332).** The prototype's palette (`Dialogs.tsx:4380-4420`, retired) read a query that is a year, optionally followed by a period of that year; when the year was inside the served year bounds of the period grammar it put one entry first, "go to" that period, and choosing it set the page's period and restarted the paged window; outside the bounds it offered nothing. Production page actions are static labels (`shell/actions.ts:25-49`) registered through `components/ledger/useLedgerPage.ts:342`, so a typed year finds nothing. Production must validate and name the period through the backend's served periods and their bounds (`usePeriods`, as `components/ledger/PeriodSelector.tsx` does), never a client regex of period codes or a year constant. Choosing between a query-aware action provider in the shell palette (`components/CommandPalette.tsx`) and quiet per-year actions built from the years-with-movements read is an implementation choice within the shell ADR; years without movements inside the served bounds must still be reachable, as R21 allows any year. Prototype story: "Command palette, searching a year". Acceptance: a test types a year and a year with a period, chooses the entry and sees the page's period change; an out-of-bounds year offers nothing.

**P05.S26 - several movements' model suggestions as a reviewed flow (audit R5, lines 168-171; M5, lines 416-420; P04 model-review-subject-lease, line 1201).** R5 requires that "Ask the model for each" lists the suggestions one at a time, so they are approved together only after they have been read. Production (`components/ledger/inspector/SelectionInspector.tsx:121`, `458-522`, `539-544`, `911-990`) asks up to `ASK_EACH_MAX` visible movements one review at a time, which is correct, but then lists every answer at once inline in the selection inspector, with an Approve button on each and "Approve all n" available immediately. The gap is exactly: no one-at-a-time reading, no gate that every suggestion was read before approving together, no way to leave one out, and no share entry for a Mixed suggestion without a share (production excludes it, which meets M5's "never saved with the share empty" but not "Enter a share to approve"). The prototype's dialog (`Dialogs.tsx:2690-2850`, opened from `App.tsx:594-625`, retired) was modal: a title, a lead with the count, the served model name, that it ran on this computer and that each is read before approving; previous and next icon buttons around an "i of n" polite live region; one card per suggestion with the description, amount and currency, counterparty and date, then the suggested classification (with its share, or that none was proposed), category, IVA category, a confidence meter with its percentage and the model's reason; a required business-share field (R3 percent grammar) for a Mixed suggestion without a share; and a "leave this one out" checkbox. The footer counted "read k of n" until every suggestion had been visited, then how many will be approved; Close; and a primary approve-together button disabled with a tooltip until every one was read. Approving staged one approval per kept suggestion with the writes M5 lists, left out Mixed suggestions without a valid share and said so; an empty result showed a one-line "no suggestions" dialog; a footnote said approvals are recorded as the model's and approved by the person, and that IVA figures come from registry rates, never from the model. Production keeps its per-movement review hold and release (the ADR's 2026-10-24 amendment), the write pause, the ask bound and the failure words (`modelFailureText`); a review the dialog shows and nobody stages is released when the dialog closes. The suggestion fields come from `LedgerLlmSuggestion` (`shell/ledgerMovementOperations.ts:338`): `reason`, `confidence`, `provenance`, `business_pct`. Prototype story: "Model suggestions for several movements". Insertion points: a new `components/ledger/inspector/SuggestionsDialog.tsx` opened by `askEach` in `SelectionInspector.tsx`, replacing the inline list. Acceptance: a scenario test asks about several movements, cannot approve before visiting each, leaves one out, enters a share for a Mixed one, approves, and sees exactly the kept approvals staged and the unstaged reviews released.

## Parallelization

P01 and P02 can run in parallel: the host channel and the grid primitive share no files. P03.S07 needs P01.S03 and P01.S04 (the port and the scenario answer) and P02.S05. P03.S08 to P03.S14 build on S07. S08 and S09 are sequential, while S10 to S14 can run in parallel once S09 lands, with disjoint files under `src/components/ledger/`. P04.S15 follows the P03 Steps whose strings it declares, and P04.S16 follows P04.S15. Shared shell files (`App.tsx`, `layout.ts`, `host.ts`, `contract.ts`) have one writer at a time. Other sessions edit the frontend concurrently, so re-read these files before each patch.

P05 order. Run P05.S22 (shortcuts sheet) and P05.S23 (draft inspector) first: they are self-contained and S22 fixes the declared chord list that the phone layout hides. P05.S21 (first run) and P05.S24 (ledger states) both edit `components/ledger/LedgerPageBody.tsx`, so they run one after the other, S24 first because the first-run test must also respect a locked or never-read ledger; S24 may need a host contract addition before its frontend work. P05.S25 (palette year search) is independent of the others and can run beside them; it touches the shell palette, which has one writer at a time. P05.S26 (reviewed suggestions dialog) is independent of S20 to S25 and owns `components/ledger/inspector/SelectionInspector.tsx`. P05.S20 (phone width) runs last: it changes `MovementsSection.tsx`, `LedgerHeader.tsx`, `RightPanel.tsx`, `ReviewDialog.tsx` and `PeriodSelector.tsx`, hides the S22 button, and must lay out the S21 checklist, the S23 draft inspector and the S24 states at phone width. Each P05 Step declares its strings through P04.S15's catalogue in the same Step. A review at the P05 close follows the plan's review rule.
