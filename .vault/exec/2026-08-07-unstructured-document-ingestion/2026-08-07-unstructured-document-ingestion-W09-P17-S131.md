# Scope the territory grounding assertions to the article and read the same artefact the catalogue gate reads, since the statute phrases are currently asserted against the entire six-hundred-thousand-character law rather than against article 3, which is non-vacuous today only because each phrase happens to occur exactly once, and a consolidation repeating an exclusion phrase elsewhere would silently convert the discriminating check into a passing one. The silence assertion about the Balearic Islands is the mirror problem and will red on any unrelated future amendment mentioning them, with a message pointing at territorial scope. Separately the test reads the markdown sidecar while the catalogue reference resolves through the json sidecar, so two artefacts regenerated by different paths can diverge and leave the gate green over bytes nobody checked. Resolving the anchored unit closes both at once. Also correct the exec record character count, which states 2733 against a measured 2311

## Scope

- `src/cadrumo/domain/iva`

## Description

- Resolve the cited article from the catalogue entry's own `corpus_ref`,
  against the json sidecar, with the resolver the registry evidence gate uses.
- Scope every statute assertion to that article unit instead of the whole law.
- Parse the legal catalogues as TOML rather than searching them as text, and
  require the cited entry to carry a `corpus_ref` at all.
- Add a case pinning that the citation resolves to one article, at both ends.
- Report a failed statute check by phrase rather than by corpus operand.
- Correct the character count recorded for the bundled article unit.

## Outcome

Both findings close on the same change, and the reason they are one change is
that both are the same defect: an assertion whose discriminating power came
from how narrow its subject happened to be rather than from how it was written.
Read law-wide, "the exclusion limb is present" is true while the phrase exists
anywhere in six hundred thousand characters, and "the Balears are unnamed" is a
claim about the entire statute that the table never rested on and could not keep
true. Scoped to the article the citation actually names, both say what the
territory table actually depends on.

The scope is now asserted rather than assumed. A resolver regression handing
back the whole document would satisfy every positive check in the module while
checking almost nothing, so a case pins the article's own rubric present and
wording from a different article of the same law absent. The negative half was
chosen against a phrase that genuinely occurs in the law, so it discriminates.

Reading the artefact the catalogue reads was the third gap and needed no
separate work: resolving through `corpus_ref` reaches the json sidecar by
construction, and the markdown read the module previously did is gone rather
than reconciled.

One defect found while proving the fix, and fixed here rather than filed. The
statute checks were written as membership expressions, which the runner renders
both sides of on failure. Under precisely the regression the new scope guard
exists to catch, one side is the whole consolidated law, and printing the
failure took longer than a four-minute ceiling allowed — the gate fired and
could not say so. Every statute check now goes through a helper returning a
bool, so a failure names the phrase and never the corpus.

Three mutations, installed from outside the repository at plugin module scope so
each lands before the module under test imports the resolver. Each asserts its

First mutation, the resolver handing back the whole consolidated document

The failing case is the scope guard. The other three passing is not a gap — it
is the finding itself, reproduced: read law-wide, the exclusion and silence
checks still pass while establishing almost nothing.

Second mutation, the article losing the Ceuta and Melilla exclusion limb, its
activation line asserting the limb was present before deleting it:

    1 failed, 3 passed in 1.30s

Third mutation, the article starting to name the Balears, its activation line
asserting the article did not already name them:

    1 failed, 3 passed in 1.24s

Each mutation reddens exactly the one case that owns the property it breaks.

A discriminating probe then compares the OLD law-wide reading against the NEW
article-scoped reading on the two scenarios the review named, because a mutation
that both readings catch would not show the fix changed anything:

    SCENARIO 1 - the exclusion limb leaves art. 3 but survives elsewhere in the law
      old law-wide assertion 'limb in law'      -> True   (green: defect NOT caught)
      new article assertion  'limb in art 3'    -> False  (red: defect caught)
    SCENARIO 2 - an unrelated future amendment mentions the Balears
      old law-wide assertion 'Balears absent'   -> False  (red: FALSE positive)
      new article assertion  'Balears absent'   -> True   (green: correctly quiet)

The corrected character count was re-measured rather than taken on report: the
anchored unit's own text is 2311 characters, and rendering it with its title,
which is what the registry evidence gate reads, gives 2340.

## Notes

The three statute phrases were confirmed to occur exactly once each in the whole
law before the rewrite. That is what made the previous assertions pass, and it
is also why the earlier record could honestly show its mutations being caught:
removing a phrase from a corpus that holds one copy reds a law-wide check as
readily as an article-scoped one. The coincidence was load-bearing and invisible
from inside, which is the shape worth remembering rather than the instance.

A peer's whole-tree sweep committed this work mid-refactor, at
`ed09a6dd4b`, capturing the state after the article scoping and before the
failure-reporting fix. That intermediate is a working four-case gate — it is the
exact content verified green earlier in the session — so the tree was never red,
but it did carry the unreportable-failure defect for the length of one commit.
The completion landed under its own explicit pathspec immediately afterwards.

This gate is bespoke and its author has already nominated it for deletion in
favour of routing the IVA registry tables through the real evidence path. That
row is unowned and unscheduled, so the gate stays wrong until it is done; this
change makes it right in the meantime and deletes nothing that row would need.
