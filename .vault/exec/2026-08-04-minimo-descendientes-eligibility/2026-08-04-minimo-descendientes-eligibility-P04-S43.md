# Amend the stale blocker in the S16 record and the contradictory plan prose

- `.vault/plan/2026-08-04-minimo-descendientes-eligibility-plan.md`

## Description

- Strip the retired regional-table blocker from the S16 execution record's heading.
- Record in that record's notes why the blocker was dissolved rather than satisfied.
- Correct the plan paragraph asserting a Step was still open while its row was checked.

## Outcome

Neither document now carries a premise its own campaign had already retired.

The blocker was dissolved by research rather than met: the informative return reporting childcare custody is filed exclusively by the centre, which must apply its own region's calendar and report the resulting months to the authority. The determination was never this application's to make, and building the table would have risked contradicting a return the authority already holds.

## Notes

This mattered more than tidiness because of what the plan already said. Its own prose retires the blocker and warns in terms that it must not be reinstated from reading a row. The row was updated when the blocker retired; this record was not. So the RECORD became the surviving carrier of exactly the premise the plan warns against — and a reader who trusts records over prose, which is the ordinary reader because the record is the structured surface, would have rebuilt a precondition the campaign had measured out of existence.

The plan's own prose had drifted the same way, asserting the Step was awaiting review "which is why the row is still open" while the row read as checked.

The generalisable shape: **a correction that lands in one surface and not its siblings leaves the un-updated surface asserting the retired thing with full confidence.** Retiring a premise is not finished until every document stating it has been swept, and the structured surfaces are the ones that get believed.

Surfaced by the campaign-close honesty review, which was looking for exactly this class — a document outliving the correction that retired its premise.
