## Notes

Three gates are now blocking on every push, each of them previously reachable only by manual
dispatch. They fail only on movement, so the lane reddens for whoever causes it rather than
carrying standing debt. The denominator had no recipe at all and
gained one, because the dev-tree test lane covers the quality tests directory but not the one this
gate lives in.

THREE SIBLING GATES ARE DELIBERATELY NOT WIRED, and the omission is written into the workflow rather
than left silent. The canonical-authority fixed point is red on a duplicate destination authority
in the full-screen navigation module, re-measured immediately before this step and unchanged at
eight violations. The unused-symbol ratchet is red on three orphaned-test entries whose own lane
has already resolved them without shrinking the baseline. Wiring either would fail every push in
every lane on a defect the wiring change cannot fix, which is the opposite of what a per-push gate
is for. Each carries its reopening condition, so both remain scheduled work for their owners
instead of disappearing.

The fixed-point exclusion is required rather than merely prudent, and the plan says so itself: a
step that registers an operation must update the dispatch table and the fixed-point authority
atomically with it. Wiring that gate red would therefore block every operation registration in the
reachability and editor waves on a violation this campaign neither caused nor owns.

THE STEP'S PREMISE WAS PARTLY FALSE. It names the architecture check among the gates reachable only
by manual dispatch; that check has been wired as its own workflow step all along. The workflow
comment records that it is already present and not repeated, so a later reader does not add it
twice.

One defect of this campaign's own making was found by measuring for this step rather than by the
step that caused it. Relocating a projection into a reachable module left a public export nothing
imports, which the unconsumed-export ratchet reports and the relocation's own verification could
not have seen. It was corrected before wiring, and the lesson is recorded: after a relocation, run
the ratchets that scope on reachability, because a symbol moving into a reachable module can newly
violate a gate that never examined it.

A FOURTH GATE WAS WIRED AND THEN WITHDRAWN WITHIN THE HOUR, and that is the most useful thing this
step learned. The unconsumed-export ratchet was measured green, wired, and re-measured red, because
a concurrent lane published two names nothing imports in the interval. Nothing about this campaign
changed between the two readings.

The criterion this step began with -- wire what is green and can change state -- is therefore too
weak, and the corrected one is recorded in the workflow: a gate is wirable only if it is STABLY
green. Seven worktrees are live against this repository, so a point-in-time reading says nothing
about the next push, and wiring on one converts another lane's ordinary churn into a repository-wide
block. The withdrawal is recorded rather than quietly reverted, because the mistake is instructive
and the gate becomes wirable the moment the full-screen lane stops carrying unconsumed exports.
