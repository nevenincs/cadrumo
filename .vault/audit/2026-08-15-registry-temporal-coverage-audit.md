# `registry-temporal-coverage` audit: `coverage instrument close-out and operator summary`

## Scope

A fresh-context close-out of tonight's coverage-instrument work: two hard-fail gates over the modelo registry's declared revision spans (`test_every_modelo_revision_span_is_corpus_proven`, the relayout-crossing instrument; `test_every_modelo_resolves_exactly_one_revision_for_every_filing_year_through_today`, the coverage-hole instrument), both re-verified at current HEAD immediately before this document was written. The question this audit answers for the operator, without requiring code reading: how much of the registry can prove it has not silently drifted across an AEAT record-design re-layout, what stands between here and more, and what the operator personally has to decide or obtain. Both gates read the bundled registry through the raw loader, never `ValidatedRegistryAuthority`, which currently refuses to build over an unrelated export-layout-completeness gate -- a pre-existing, out-of-scope condition, unchanged by anything below.

## Findings

### how-much-can-prove-coverage | high | 12 of 97 declared revisions are corpus-proven clean; 85 fail, honestly

**"Prove" means one specific thing here, and it is worth stating precisely: a revision's declared filing-year span is CORPUS-PROVEN only when a comparison against real bundled AEAT record-design evidence positively finds no undetected re-layout inside it, never when the absence of a comparison is silent.** A revision the instrument has not been able to compare is a FAILURE naming why, never a pass -- there is no third "unproven, assumed fine" state anywhere in this instrument, by explicit operator ruling.

**3 of 97 revisions could be proven clean before tonight's work began; 0 of 73 modelos had every one of their revisions proven. 12 of 97 revisions can be proven clean now, and 2 of 73 modelos (131, 390) now have EVERY declared revision proven -- confirmed by a fresh run at HEAD.** 85 of 97 revisions, and 71 of 73 modelos, still have at least one unproven revision. This is not a comfortable number and it is not supposed to be one -- it is the honest state of the registry's temporal-coverage evidence, measured, not asserted.

**The 9-revision increase is real new capability, not a corpus change, and it is what moved 131 and 390 from partially-proven to fully-proven.** Before tonight, a revision declaring exactly one filing year (a `2020`-style span, never a range) could not pass this gate under ANY circumstance, regardless of how much AEAT evidence existed for it -- the original instrument could only prove a span clean by comparing two YEARS WITHIN it, and a one-year span has no second year to compare. That is a structural defect, the same shape the operator's ruling on "no undecidable verdict" was aimed at: a gate that can never say yes to a true claim, no matter the evidence, has re-created a soft failure state under a harder name. Tonight's fix adds a second, complementary proof: a single-year revision proves itself by differing from its adjacent revision's design instead of from itself. 9 revisions -- all of Modelo 131's, Modelo 303's, and Modelo 390's single-year spans -- now pass under this branch, each backed by real, named, corroborated evidence (five of the nine on box-numbered signals, four entirely on box-free ones, none resting solely on the newest, least-exercised signal). Modelo 131's and Modelo 390's single-year revisions were EXACTLY their remaining unproven ones, so closing this structural gap is what made those two modelos fully provable; Modelo 303 also gained single-year passes but still carries proven-gap revisions elsewhere in its span, so it is not fully proven. **These 9 revisions, and the 2 modelos they complete, are the entire increase in what this registry can prove tonight.**

### zero-corpus-driven-passes | high | Every OTHER change tonight moved a failure's REASON, never its VERDICT

Separate from the 9 single-year passes above, five other changes landed in the coverage instrument tonight: a description-flip fix recovering silently-discarded evidence, a sixth comparison signal (box-free field-set membership), an offset-aware coverage-hole horizon, an absent-versus-unparseable distinction, and -- landed by a different agent in the same file -- a title-based design-attribution mechanism that gave 11 previously-invisible modelos at least one readable design. **Diffed by revision NAME against a clean pre-attribution baseline, not by comparing aggregate counts: zero revisions moved from failing to passing as a result of any of these five.** Two revisions (Modelo 190, Modelo 193) did change category -- from "needs more corpus evidence" to "proven relayout crossing" -- which is a RECLASSIFICATION onto the true cause, not progress toward a passing state. Both were already-failing revisions; both are still failing revisions; only the stated reason, and the concrete remedy it implies, changed. Read "we improved the coverage gate tonight" as "we made five failing revisions state their true cause instead of an approximate one", never as "the registry gained coverage."

### what-stands-between-here-and-more | high | Four distinct populations, each needing a different action from a different owner

The 85 failing revisions, and the modelos behind them, split into populations that do NOT share a remedy. Folding any two together sends the operator (or whoever acts on this) to do the wrong thing.

1. **17 modelos have literally no bundled AEAT record design at all** (121, 136, 140, 143, 179, 186, 231, 233, 234, 238, 289, 361, 379, 380, 592, 721, 848), confirmed by filesystem directory absence, not a parse failure. The only fix is acquiring the design from AEAT. 14 of the 17 are open-ended spans, so the acquisition need spans potentially the whole declared window, not one year. Full per-modelo needed-year detail is in `[[2026-08-15-registry-temporal-coverage-acquisition-worklist-research]]`.
2. **35 modelos carry a bundled, readable design but at least one revision still needs more corpus evidence -- enrolment work, not acquisition, for most of them.** Measured directly rather than repeated from an earlier figure: of the 46 modelos outside the acquisition-17, non-ejercicio-9, and Modelo 036, only 11 have EVERY declared revision already resolved by the existing corpus (either proven clean or a proven relayout crossing needing a split, not new evidence): 131, 190, 193, 200, 220, 322, 347, 353, 390, 604, 714. The other **35** have at least one revision still short of two comparable design years: 100, 111, 115, 117, 122, 123, 126, 128, 130, 151, 156, 165, 180, 181, 182, 184, 185, 187, 188, 189, 194, 202, 216, 222, 232, 270, 280, 296, 303, 308, 309, 341, 345, 349, 490. An earlier verbal figure of 29 for this population was not corroborated by this direct count and should be discarded in favour of the 35 measured here; the two counts were never re-derived against each other before tonight and the earlier one is superseded. Whether each of the 35 needs a genuinely new AEAT acquisition or already holds a second comparable design under a still-unattributed file was not established tonight -- see Recommendations.
3. **9 modelos carry bundled design files that parse without error and state no ejercicio anywhere, in either filename or content** (038, 145, 210, 360, 369, 576, 720, 763, 840). This is a genuine, legitimate answer for some of them -- a design scoped by an in-force date or a devengo span rather than an ejercicio -- not a defect. These need a REGISTRY-SIDE DECISION (does this modelo's coverage get validated by a different instrument shape than year-to-year comparison) rather than either acquisition or enrolment. Modelo 720 is notable here: independently measured as "one attestation act from filable" earlier this campaign, and simultaneously blocked on this axis -- the two questions are separate and a taxpayer-facing "can this file" answer needs both resolved.
4. **1 modelo, 036, is a mixed case the gate now names automatically rather than requiring a hand-check.** Two of its five files are legitimately non-ejercicio-scoped (same shape as population 3). The other three carry a real, filename-derivable ejercicio year but their sheets fail to parse ENTIRELY -- an extraction-layer defect, being worked by a different agent concurrently, not an acquisition or enrolment task. The coverage gate's two failure messages now distinguish this shape ("UNPARSEABLE, not absent") from a genuine acquisition gap wherever the two could otherwise be confused; bite-proofed synthetically since Modelo 036's own declared span currently does not overlap its three unparseable years, so the branch is proven-by-construction and presently dormant in the live corpus -- prospective, not speculative, since the three files still exist and a future span change brings them back into range.

### coverage-hole-gate-clean | medium | The second gate passes cleanly: zero holes, zero overlaps, tree-wide

Separately from the relayout-crossing gate above, the coverage-hole gate (does every modelo resolve exactly one revision for every filing year through today) passes with zero holes and zero overlaps, confirmed fresh at HEAD. The gate's horizon is offset-aware: an annual-in-arrears modelo (Renta, the annual IVA summary, Patrimonio, and others -- derived from the modelo's own declared period cadence, never a separately-stored field) is only required to resolve through LAST year, since this year's filing window has not opened yet; a periodic modelo is required to resolve through THIS year. Bite-proofed both directions before trusting it: a constructed periodic revision missing its current year still fails; a constructed annual-in-arrears revision missing only the not-yet-due current year correctly does not.

### two-views-no-shared-authority | medium | The coverage gates and the export generator read the corpus through two unrelated routes, and nothing reconciles them

This instrument's two gates read the bundled corpus directly off the filesystem (`rglob` over `disenos_registro/modelo_<id>/files/`) and never consult the registry's `sources` catalogue or a revision's `source_refs`. The export-layout generator reads the opposite way: it addresses a design exclusively through a `record_design`-kind source declared in the registry, hash-pinned by `corpus_path`/`sha256`/`bytes`, tagged with a `record_design_epoch` -- per its own owning team, "this is how a design is addressed at all" for that consumer. A bundled file present on disk is therefore SIMULTANEOUSLY visible to one route and invisible to the other, depending only on which one is asked, and nothing in the registry today declares or checks that the two views agree. Confirmed directly: of 97 declared revisions, 45-46 already have a `record_design`- or legacy `form_spec`-kind AEAT source declared; the remaining ~51-52 do not, and of those, 21 have a bundled file whose attributed year genuinely falls inside the revision's own declared need (enrollable today, provenance fully recoverable -- see the reconciliation below), 17 have no bundled file at all (the acquisition list), and the remainder have a file present but attributed to years outside what that specific revision needs (a genuine mismatch, not evidence to enrol). This gap is worth recording as its own finding rather than folding into either gate's number, because a future change to either route (the gate's attribution pipeline, or the generator's enrollment convention) can silently drift from the other with no test today that would catch it.

## Recommendations

### acquire-the-17 | Operator action: obtain AEAT record designs for the 17 zero-corpus modelos

The only population in this audit whose fix requires something the operator, not this codebase, must obtain. Per-modelo needed years are in `[[2026-08-15-registry-temporal-coverage-acquisition-worklist-research]]`. No engineering change closes this population; it is a pure evidence-acquisition task.

### decide-the-nine | Registry-authoring decision needed for the 9 legitimately non-ejercicio modelos (plus 2 of Modelo 036's 5 files)

Whether year-to-year design comparison is the right coverage instrument for a modelo whose AEAT design is genuinely not ejercicio-scoped is a decision this audit does not make. A follow-on ADR should rule on whether these modelos need a distinct validation shape (e.g. comparing by in-force date or devengo span instead of ejercicio), or whether they are correctly excluded from this instrument's claim entirely with an explicit, cited registry declaration -- never a silent allowlist.

### measure-the-eleven | Distinguish enrolment from acquisition inside the 11 attribution-gained modelos

For each of the ten modelos that gained exactly one comparable year (156, 165, 180, 181, 187, 188, 189, 194, 270, 280), determine whether a second comparable design year is already present in the bundled corpus under a different, still-unattributed file, or whether it genuinely requires a new AEAT acquisition. This was not established tonight and is the natural next measurement before committing operator time to acquisition that enrolment could resolve instead.

### track-the-extraction-fix | Confirm Modelo 036's extraction fix closes the UNPARSEABLE branch when it lands

A concurrent agent is making partial parser reads representable rather than silent. When that work reaches Modelo 036's three currently-unparseable files, re-run this gate and confirm they move to a real, corpus-proven verdict (pass or a genuine PROVEN GAP) rather than remaining UNPARSEABLE. The gate's distinction (this audit's finding four) is what will make that transition visible as a verdict change rather than a silent disappearance.
