## Scope

A fresh-context consolidation of tonight's legal-grounding vein, distinct from the corpus-design coverage work in `2026-08-15-registry-temporal-coverage-audit`. That audit asks whether a revision's declared span is proven against bundled AEAT record-design evidence; this one asks whether the LEGAL citations grounding those revisions -- and the values, deadlines and layouts they claim to authorise -- actually say what the registry claims they say. Every finding below was independently measured this session, not carried forward from an earlier report. Where a finding rests on inference rather than direct measurement, that is stated in the same line, per the standing per-item evidence-class discipline this vein used throughout.

## Findings

### four-confirmed-defects | high | Four modelos have a proven legal-grounding defect, each a DIFFERENT shape, each needing a DIFFERENT remedy

Grouping these by symptom would produce a wrong fix for at least two of the four. Each is graded by evidence class.

1. **M714 (Modelo de Patrimonio) -- unverifiable citation, `valid_to`/`year_to` closed on no legal basis. Evidence class: unverifiable / stopped.** The sole citation for M714's revision, `orden-hac-1023-2021:modelo-714`, fails on TWO independent axes: the bundled corpus file is an 859-byte title-and-summary stub with no operative article of its own, and the cited `document_id` (`BOE-A-2021-7593`) resolves -- confirmed twice, via two different BOE URL forms -- to an unrelated Ayuntamiento de Benetússer police-vacancy resolution, not this orden at all. A structural doubt beyond the citation itself: external search suggests AEAT's real pattern for this tax may be one orden per ejercicio (matching M100/M303's shape), not one perpetual open-ended orden, which would mean M714's whole revision architecture, not just its bound, needs re-examination. **Stopped rather than widened** -- the standing rule (never ground a filing-relevant date on a citation that may have been authored alongside the phrase validating it) applied exactly as designed.
2. **M151 (Modelo de IRPF impatriados) -- stub grounding. Evidence class: internal, mechanically confirmed.** `orden-eha-2887-2008:modelo-151` is a 915-byte paraphrase with no operative article of its own (only prose cross-references to Ley 35/2006 art 93 and RD 439/2007 arts 113-120). Caught by the same calibrated dispositive-content detector built for this exact shape (see the stub-grounding gate below); the gate now refuses this at registry load.
3. **M220's 2022 record-design edition -- corpus defect, not a legal-citation defect.** Reported by tier1: 3 blank type cells in the 2022 record-design file, zero in 2023/24/25. Included here only because it shares the same acquisition queue as the three legal-grounding items; it is an acquisition item for the operator, not a code fix.
4. **M185 -- 9 of 12 monthly deadline windows cite an orden that had not yet taken legal effect when those windows opened. Evidence class: internal contradiction, convention-independent.** `orden-hac-1197-2025:art-4`'s own declared `effective_from = 2025-10-22` postdates windows `modelo-185-2025-01` through `-09` (opening Feb-Oct 2025); only windows `-10/-11/-12` (opening after the orden's effective date) are clean -- the boundary falling exactly where the cause predicts is itself a control. No predecessor orden exists anywhere in the catalogue for M185. Unlike M714/M151, **the citation itself is genuine** -- read directly, `orden-hac-1197-2025.html` carries real "Articulo 1. Aprobacion..."/"Articulo 4. Plazo de presentacion..." content, confirmed real during the 26-file risk-tier read. M185 is thin (only two articles excerpted, not the full orden) but not wrong about what it says -- it is wrong only about which period it can authorise. **Single-axis defect**, the cleaner of the four to fix: no question about the text's authenticity, only the dates need reconciling (or the real predecessor authority needs finding).

A fifth, closely related but SEPARATE finding: `valid_from` on M200's revision was corrected `2025-01-01 -> 2024-01-01` this session (landed, verified against the real load) -- established by internal corroboration alone (sibling M220 under the identical orden; the revision's own `filing_year=2024` deadline window inside a `valid_from=2025` revision is a self-contradiction needing no external authority). This is now fixed, not an open item.

### seven-layout-acquisition-candidates | medium | Legal text establishes a record layout for 7 of the 17 zero-corpus-file modelos; the rest are undecidable, not disproven

Of the 17 modelos with zero bundled `disenos_registro/` files (`2026-08-15-registry-temporal-coverage-audit`'s population 1), checked each one's cited legal text for language establishing a physical/logical layout ("diseños físicos y lógicos", "su anexo de contenido") rather than inferring from file absence.

**Strong (explicit "diseños físicos y lógicos" or content-anexo language, evidence class: sibling-corroborated legal text):** M721 (`orden-hfp-886-2023:art-1`, verbatim "...y sus diseños físicos y lógicos"), M186 (`orden-hac-539-2003:art-1`, same phrasing), M289 (`orden-hap-1695-2016:art-1`+`art-6`, content-anexo plus format remitted to the AEAT sede -- the same shape most already-bundled designs in this corpus come from -- corroborated by 13 further amending ordenes 2018-2025 all modifying that same anexo), M231 (`orden-hfp-1978-2016:art-1`, content-anexo, amended by `orden-hac-1285-2020`), M379 (`orden-hfp-1415-2023` arts 1/3/5, explicit electronic-format/anexo language).

**Weaker (a real anexo exists but reads closer to a printable form than a machine-readable design, evidence class: analogy-only):** M361, M136.

**Genuinely undecidable (10 of 17): 121, 140, 143, 179, 233, 234, 238, 380, 592, 848. Evidence class: excerpt-absence, not law-absence.** Each cites only 3 legal_refs, and every one of the 10 founding ordenes bundled for them is a self-declared excerpt (1022-2182 bytes, each carrying the identical closing line "[verbatim] excerpt transcribed from the BOE consolidated text ... Pending operator re-verification", confirmed on 2 direct reads and inferred by identical size/structure for the remaining 8 rather than read individually, per the stop-rather-than-repeat-thoroughness-theatre call). **This is the same excerpt-absence trap the campaign was founded on, one level deeper**: a missing design FILE is not proof the law never established one; a missing PHRASE in a deliberately partial excerpt is not proof the full orden never established one either. None of the 10 is confirmed to lack a layout; none is confirmed to have one. Closing this needs either the full consolidated text for each of the 10 ordenes, or a direct AEAT Diseño de Registro index check per modelo number -- both acquisition-class actions.

### no-corpus-tier-in-schema | high | Neither `SourceReference` nor `LegalReference` records anything about a citation's completeness

The structural finding underneath every stub, excerpt and undecidable modelo above. `corpus_ref`/`corpus_path` is a bare string; nothing on the model distinguishes a 1.9MB full consolidated text from a 900-byte paraphrase until a human, or a targeted check, opens the file. Every grounding check in the system -- `required_text` presence, `verify_legal_reference_grounding`, the stub-grounding gate -- currently treats both as equally authoritative evidence.

**The clearest demonstration: M151 and M216 sit 470 bytes apart with opposite verdicts.** `orden-eha-2887-2008.html` (M151, 915 bytes) is a confirmed stub with no operative article; `orden-eha-3290-2008.html` (M216, 1385 bytes) is confirmed genuinely real, correctly-structured content. No size threshold, and no automated heuristic tried this session, could have sorted that pair correctly -- reading was the only route, on this pair and on all 26 files checked in the wider risk-tier sweep (0 stubs found among the 26; every one was genuinely real content, just short because its orden genuinely has few articles).

**The shape a fix would take, named without being built:** a closed tier field (`full_consolidated` / `provision_excerpt` -- deliberately never a third, declarable "stub" value; a paraphrase with no operative text should only ever be a build-time refusal, never a legal state to assert) on `LegalReference`/`SourceReference`, set at authoring time and validated at load against the corpus's own observed size/filename-shape population (measured this session: provision-suffixed filenames median 2249 bytes, bare full-text filenames run 5KB-3MB). A consumer grounding a filing-grade value could then require `full_consolidated`, or a `provision_excerpt` whose own text independently passes the dispositive-content check, and refuse anything else. Whether this is worth building is an operator call, alongside the `valid_from` semantics question below.

### denominator-was-half-the-catalogue | high | The legal catalogue is 1303 entries, not 633 -- every share reported tonight was scoped to the authored half

Every percentage in this vein (`agent_reviewed`/`operator_reviewed` split, the 191-entry provisional-marker count, the value-bearing surface counts) was measured against the 633-entry compiler-tier catalogue (`load_registry_tree`). **Independently confirmed** -- calling `_construct_authority` directly (a structurally different code path from re-reading the compiler tier a second time, avoiding the exact circularity tier0 flagged in its own first pass) -- **the AUTHORITY-tier catalogue is 1303 entries: 633 authored, 670 synthesised, 0 lost either direction.**

The 670 are `Modelo.M303`'s régimen simplificado annual IVA módulos activity index, one legal ref per activity per year, compiled from 5 real bundled annual "orden de módulos" documents (2021-2025, each 467-507KB, confirmed Tier 1 full consolidated text -- several times larger than any other file checked this session). **Traced and confirmed sound engineering, not exposure:** all 670 resolve cleanly through the real production grounding path (`verify_legal_reference_grounding`, the exact function every hand-authored citation is checked through -- an initial mechanical anchor check gave a false 100% failure, caught before reporting, because the target files are raw BOE page scrapes with no literal per-provision HTML ids; the REAL resolver, which reads through the `.extracted.json` sidecar, succeeds on all 670). Provenance is honestly distinct too: `reviewed_by = 'compiler:m303-annual-orden-html-v5'`, a versioned machine identity, not a claimed human review -- correctly, honestly unreviewed, because nothing was authored for a human to review.

**Traced to a genuine production consumer**, not a test-only shape -- distinguished explicitly, since this is exactly where the campaign's two prior false alarms (`resolve_record_design_binary`, the `algorithm_bindings` family) hid: `resolve_m303_regimen_simplificado_snapshot` reads the compiled `M303AnnualOrdenAuthority.activities` directly, **not** through the generic `legal_refs`/binding/casilla graph -- which is why an earlier check finding zero casilla/binding citations against the 670 was correct and remains correct; it was measuring a different, real mechanism, not a wrong answer. That resolver is called from `application/modelo/_m303_filing_evidence.py`'s filing-evidence validator, imported by four production modules including two CLI entrypoints -- `_app_quickfile.py` and `_modelo_work_calculate_cli.py`, the live `calculate`/`quickfile` surface. **Verdict: sound engineering, consumed to the CLI surface, provenance metadata riding beside a real numeric path** -- a dedicated typed projection instead of the generic binding mechanism, a legitimate architectural choice for a modelo whose annual módulos index does not fit the binding shape, not a gap.

**Denominator ruling, both numbers correct, one is the meaningful metric:**

- **220 of 633 -- 35% -- is the review-quality metric, and it is the number that carries meaning.** It answers "of the entries a human could meaningfully have reviewed, how many did an operator attest". This is the figure every earlier measurement this session correctly used, and it needs no re-run -- the 633 denominator was right the whole time for this question.
- **220 of 1303 -- 17% -- is catalogue composition, not a review deficit.** It answers "what fraction of ALL entries carry an operator signature", which lumps in 670 machine-derived rows where operator review was never expected, never appropriate, and whose absence signals nothing. Quoting 17% as though it were the truer number implies a review gap that does not exist.

**Nothing measured earlier this session needs re-running.** Every prior percentage in this vein keeps its 633 denominator and stays correct as reported. Both figures are worth recording -- 1303 total, 670 machine-derived and sound, 633 authored and 35% operator-attested -- but 35% is the figure that answers the question anyone asking about grounding trust actually means.

### measured-and-rejected | medium | Four approaches were built, measured against the real population, and abandoned -- recorded so nobody rebuilds them

- **A coverage-ratio screen and an identifier-agreement gate** (tier0): both measured against the real legal corpus and found to have zero discriminating power. A fabricated artefact is internally consistent by construction, and both of these measure internal consistency; a wrong `document_id` propagated into four files, all agreeing with each other. Internal consistency cannot detect fabrication.
- **Provisional-marker mining** (`reviewed_by` free-text scan for "pending"/"to re-stamp"/etc): measured against all 633 authored entries. 191 carry a marker, but only 2 of the 5 confirmed defects do -- M714, M151 and M200's citation carry the bare, unmarked `'agent-review'` string, textually indistinguishable from the other 141 unmarked entries beside them. **The correlation is with the review grade, not the free-text annotation.** A screen built on marker text would have missed 60% of what was actually found wrong.
- **A per-article extension of the stub-grounding gate**: authorized conditionally on the 26-file risk-tier population containing at least one real stub reached via a non-`#modelo-<id>` anchor. Read all 26; zero stubs found. The population the extension would catch is empty, so it was not built.
- **A mechanical excerpt-provision-presence check** across the 326 provision-suffixed files: 271 mechanically checked, 0 missing the provision their filename claims. 6 apparent filename/anchor disagreements were flagged by a first-pass script, then all 6 were read individually rather than trusted -- every one was a BOE-internal fragment-id convention or a Spanish ordinal word the comparator didn't recognise, never a real mismatch. The presence-versus-ownership gap tier0's attribution audit found at the modelo level does not appear to exist at the provision level in this corpus.

### landed-this-session | medium | Three production changes, all additive, all verified against the real load

1. **Stub-grounding refusal**, `verify_legal_reference_grounding` in `src/cadrumo/domain/calculations/registry/_legal.py` -- refuses a dispositive-kind legal reference anchored at `#modelo-<id>` whose corpus text carries no article/disposición of its own. Scoped to this one anchor shape deliberately: a first attempt scoped to every dispositive-kind reference mis-fired on 59 of 633 real, correctly-grounded entries (apartados, anexos, compound-ordinal disposiciones, bis/ter/decies-suffixed articles all legitimately lack an "articulo N." heading). Refusal set on the real catalogue: exactly `{orden-eha-2887-2008:modelo-151, orden-hac-1023-2021:modelo-714}`. Mutation-proven with a cause-unique marker string.
2. **A third failure reason**, `NO LEGAL EVIDENCE OF REVISION RECORDED`, in `test_every_modelo_revision_span_is_corpus_proven` (`test_revision_span_matches_published_designs.py`) -- fires alongside (never replacing) the existing design-evidence relayout failure when a modelo's entire revision history cites only its founding orden despite corpus evidence proving a relayout. Fires on 10 revisions across 8 modelos (193, 200, 220, 303 x3, 322, 353, 604, 714), verified strictly additive (the gate's `AssertionError` still fires; nothing that passed before still passes now by a weaker route).
3. **`valid_from` correction**, M200's revision, `2025-01-01 -> 2024-01-01` -- the only registry-data fact fix landed this session beyond two narrowly-authorized stale-comment corrections (M222, M270). Blast radius measured both in simulation and against the real load: nothing resolves differently, two previously-refusing `on=`-dated selections now resolve to the same single existing revision.

## Recommendations

### operator-acquisition-queue | Four confirmed items, ranked by what's needed

M714 (find the real governing orden(s) -- possibly one-per-ejercicio, not one perpetual orden; the existing citation is wrong, not just thin), M151 (find and bundle the real consolidated text for `orden-eha-2887-2008`), M220's 2022 record-design edition (re-acquire; 3 corrupted type cells), M185 (find the real predecessor orden governing Jan-Sep 2025, or confirm none exists and the filing basis for those months needs a different citation).

### operator-layout-decision | Seven candidates plus ten undecidable, both needing the same acquisition-class action

M721/M186/M289/M231/M379 (strong legal-text evidence a layout exists) and M361/M136 (weaker) can be prioritised for acquisition with real legal grounding already in hand. The 10 undecidable modelos (121, 140, 143, 179, 233, 234, 238, 380, 592, 848) need either the full consolidated BOE text for their founding ordenes or a direct AEAT Diseño de Registro index check -- current excerpts cannot answer the question either way.

### operator-schema-decision | Whether to build a declared corpus-tier field

Named above, not built. Would make "is this citation's evidence a full text or an excerpt" a load-time-checkable property instead of a fact only discoverable by reading the file.

### operator-valid-from-decision | Whether `valid_from` means ejercicio-start or orden-effective-date

Two internally-consistent, never-reconciled conventions coexist in this registry (Jan-1-of-ejercicio: M100/M220/M280/M289/M345/M390/M200-fixed; orden's own effective date: M182/M184/M347/M714/M720/M721/M840, five of seven `operator_reviewed`). Neither is documented as canonical. No gate was built enforcing either universally -- doing so would refuse the other convention's entire population, the same slander-a-correct-file risk found elsewhere tonight at the semantic level rather than the regex level.

### operator-denominator-decision | Which share-of-grounding figure to quote going forward

**Ruled, not open: quote 35% (220 of 633 authored entries) as the review-quality metric.** 17% (220 of 1303) is catalogue composition -- it correctly describes the full catalogue's shape but implies a review deficit that does not exist, since the 670-entry gap is machine-derived-and-sound population where operator review was never appropriate. Record both numbers for completeness (1303 total, 670 sound and machine-derived, 633 authored at 35% operator-attested), but 35% is the figure that answers what "how much of this registry's legal grounding has a human actually checked" means in practice.
