## Split and merge

Splitting requires an active, non-finalized parent, at least two nonzero children, nonnegative child magnitudes whose exact sum equals the parent, and unique child IDs. Children inherit direction and currency; ordinary splits default them to unclassified and do not clone evidence or attachment links. The parent becomes SPLIT, child/parent lineage cross-references the cohort, and one event plus the new catalogue is saved under a revision witness. If the parent has a stored EUR conversion, children use its exact FX rate and the final child absorbs the cents residual so their euro values exactly re-sum to the parent's value. A separate classified-split action builds and validates all child patches and evidence before one atomic save, refusing any patch that changes a child's content-addressed movement ID. Merge accepts only the complete active child cohort from one split group, resolves a unique SPLIT parent, and refuses if the parent or any child is cited by a finalized calculation. It archives the parent and children, creates a fresh active but unclassified row, preserves the parent amount/currency and FX facts, and writes one merge event atomically. Incomplete cohorts and pre-existing merged-ID collisions are explicit refusals; archived source rows and retained lineage support auditability. Evidence batch ingestion handles direct files and the direct files inside submitted directories. ## Assessment and follow-up

Across these modules, the strongest controls are explicit profile/operation binding at registered boundaries, revision-guarded transaction/event persistence, finalized-calculation guards, typed refusal envelopes, and content-based identity for replayable work. Split/merge preserves exact monetary totals and lineage instead of silently rounding or overwriting prior rows. The batch path is deliberately resumable and isolates ordinary per-document failures. These controls depend on the runtime adapters and callers honoring the declared ports and authority lease; this report does not prove that wiring.

There is a known post-commit boundary in manual creation/editing: transaction plus event persistence precedes attachment-manifest back-reference updates. A manifest failure can therefore leave the canonical transaction durable with its attachment manifest link behind. The add executor preserves uncertainty as UNKNOWN; the attachment executor has a PARTIAL outcome for its known attach-specific follow-on failure. Verify downstream recovery/reconciliation and operator messaging for that state. Post-commit back-reference direction (`src/cadrumo/application/ledger/actions_manual.py`) Add effect handling (`src/cadrumo/application/ledger/add_operation.py`) Attach partial-effect handling (`src/cadrumo/application/ledger/attachment_mutation_operation.py`)

The batch plan reads each source once to hash/probe it, then re-reads bytes for evidence ingestion; the expected digest is the control that should refuse changed content, and its enforcement belongs to the evidence service. `before_item` can revoke authority between stages and intentionally propagates rather than converting that global gate into a document refusal; a stop after durable items relies on rerun/idempotency to recover the final summary. The split/merge services themselves perform destructive lifecycle transitions but keep source rows archived rather than deleting them. No tests were inspected or run. Static inspection cannot verify adapter atomicity, reader/provider behavior, secure storage encryption, or full CLI reachability.
